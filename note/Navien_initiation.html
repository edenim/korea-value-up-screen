Kyung Dong Navien (009450 KS)

The plant is built. Now the cash comes back.

We initiate on Kyung Dong Navien, the Korean boiler and water heater maker that earns 58% of its revenue in North America, with an Overweight rating and a target price of KRW 83,000, 37.9% above the current price. Our KOSPI-wide screen flags Navien as one of the most undervalued companies relative to the P/B its ROE and sector justify. We think the market is anchoring on a FY2025 earnings decline that was driven by tax, not operations, and is not yet pricing the free cash flow that follows the end of a two-year capex cycle.

1. The FY2025 earnings dip was tax, not business

FY2025 operating profit rose 8% while net income fell 28%. The effective tax rate jumped to 40.5% from 18.6%, driven by a swing in deferred taxes. Tax at statutory rates was 29.3% of pre-tax profit. On a normal tax rate, the earnings base is much higher than the headline suggests.

2. The capex cycle is over and free cash flow turns positive

The KRW 104.3bn Seotan plant expansion was completed in July 2026. Capex reached 10.8% of revenue in FY2025 and free cash flow (operating cash flow less capex) was KRW (91)bn. We model capex falling to 4.5% of revenue by FY2028E and free cash flow to the firm of KRW 62bn to 90bn a year from FY2027E.

3. Room to pay out, a Value-Up story not yet told

Navien paid out 12% of FY2025 earnings, against a median of 25% across the 214 non-financials in our screen, and has not filed a Value-Up plan. The stock trades 63% below the P/B our screen model implies for its ROE and sector. Our event study shows that filing a plan alone did not move prices (mean CAR 0.24%, t = 0.41), so the catalyst we watch for is an actual increase in payout funded by the new free cash flow, not the filing itself.

Key risks

  • US demand and tariffs. 58% of revenue is North American. 1H2026 margins (18.2% vs 12.0% in 1H2025) include US tariff refunds whose amount is not disclosed. We do not extrapolate them.
  • Slowing growth. 1H2026 revenue grew 7.4% including the Commax acquisition, and Q2 revenue fell 1.0% year on year (DART preliminary results, 11 Aug 2026).
  • Share price signal. The stock fell from KRW 87,800 at end 2024 to KRW 60,200 despite higher operating profit. The market may be pricing a risk that filings do not yet show.
  • FX (KRW/USD), Russia sanctions, and losses at Commax (FY2025 operating loss KRW 14.2bn).

Market context: where the Korea discount still sits

Korea's Value-Up Program (from February 2024), the Commercial Act amendments (from July 2025) and the January 2026 dividend tax changes all aim to close the gap between Korean valuations and what companies earn. To find where that gap remains, we screened 214 KOSPI non-financials with market caps above KRW 500bn. We regress log P/B on ROE with sector fixed effects. ROE explains about half the variation (R-squared 0.53); within a sector, one extra point of ROE goes with roughly 6.0% higher P/B.

Source: DART, FinanceDataReader, analyst screen. Sector-adjusted P/B removes each sector's fixed effect; distance below the line is the discount to ROE-justified P/B.

The discount is concentrated rather than universal. Only 32% of companies trade below a textbook justified P/B at a 9.2% cost of equity. Materials, utilities and telecom, transport and autos trade well below what their ROE implies, while industrials and technology hardware trade far above it.

Source: analyst screen, ROE-only model (without sector effects) to show sector-level gaps.

Did Value-Up filings move prices? Across 169 first Value-Up plan disclosures, the mean cumulative abnormal return over trading days -1 to +5 was 0.24% (t = 0.41). Markets appear to have priced the program at the policy level, not company by company. For stock selection this means filing a plan is not enough; delivery on payout is what matters.

Why Navien. Of the 22 candidates (bottom quartile of residual, above-median ROE, below-median payout), many have peak-cycle ROE (shipbuilding) or one-off gains. Navien combines a large discount, a simple single-segment business, an explainable earnings dip and a clear cash flow inflection.

Company: a boiler maker that became a US water heater business

According to its FY2025 annual report, the parent company's sales are 45% water heaters, 38% household boilers and 17% other products. In the US, water heaters are 73% of sales. In Korea, Navien developed the industry's first condensing boiler in 1998, and condensing boilers are now mandatory in designated air-quality zones, which supports replacement demand. The company reports a single operating segment but discloses revenue by region, which we use as the forecast driver.

Source: DART notes (geographic revenue), analyst forecasts. Korea includes Commax from FY2026E.

Source: DART cash flow statements (FY2023A to FY2025A), analyst model (FY2026E onward, CFO less capex, before acquisitions and dividends).

What we assume

  • Revenue: North America +7% in FY2026E (1H2026 actual +7.1%), slowing to +4% by FY2030E. Korea flat in FY2026E (1H2026 flat even with Commax), then +3%. Russia flat, China -5%, other overseas +20% fading to +8%.
  • Margins: FY2026E uses 1H2026 as reported plus a 2H2026 margin equal to 2H2025 (7.1%). From FY2027E we use 9.5%, the FY2023 to FY2025 average, because the size of the 1H2026 tariff refund is not disclosed.
  • Tax: 29.9% on EBIT, the FY2024 to FY2025 average of tax at statutory rates, excluding deferred tax swings.
  • Reinvestment: capex 7.0% of revenue in FY2026E, 5.0% in FY2027E, 4.5% after. D&A 3.5% and net working capital 26% of revenue, in line with FY2024 to FY2025.

What would change our view

  • Margin below its FY2023 level (8.8%). At an 8.5% long-term operating margin, the DCF value is KRW 69,307 (15% upside), close to the Neutral boundary. This is the main downside case.
  • Higher discount rate. At an 8.9% WACC (one point above ours), the value is KRW 69,363 (15% upside).
  • Part of the 1H2026 margin holds. At a 10.5% long-term margin, the value is KRW 97,660 (62% upside).
  • Capital return. A Value-Up plan with a payout well above today's 12% would address the thesis directly. We would revisit the rating if free cash flow is instead spent on further acquisitions.
